## Word: đã

**Meaning**: already, used to indicate past actions or states

**Grammar**:
- **Part of Speech**: Adverb

**Usage**:
1. **Indicating Past Actions**: "Đã" is used to indicate that an action was completed in the past.
2. **Forming Affirmative Statements**: "Đã" can be used to form affirmative statements about past events.
3. **Forming Questions**: When used in questions, "đã" asks if an action has already been completed.
4. **Combining with "chưa"**: When "đã" is combined with "chưa," it forms questions that ask if an action has been completed yet.

### Examples and Explanation

1. **Indicating Past Actions**
   - **Sentence**: Tôi đã ăn sáng.
   - **English Translation**: I have already eaten breakfast.
   - **Explanation**: "Đã" indicates that the action of eating breakfast was completed in the past.

2. **Forming Affirmative Statements**
   - **Sentence**: Cô ấy đã đi làm.
   - **English Translation**: She has gone to work.
   - **Explanation**: "Đã" is used to confirm that the action of going to work has been completed.

3. **Forming Questions**
   - **Sentence**: Bạn đã xem phim này chưa?
   - **English Translation**: Have you watched this movie yet?
   - **Explanation**: "Đã" is used to ask if the action of watching the movie has already been completed. The word "chưa" at the end of the sentence makes it a question about completion.

4. **Combining with "chưa"**
   - **Sentence**: Bạn đã hoàn thành bài tập chưa?
   - **English Translation**: Have you finished your homework yet?
   - **Explanation**: "Đã" is used with "chưa" to form a question asking about the completion status of the action of finishing homework.

### Sentence Structure

- **Affirmative Statements**: Subject + đã + Verb + Object
  - **Example**: Anh ấy đã về nhà. (He has come home.)
    - **Anh ấy** (he) + **đã** (already) + **về** (come) + **nhà** (home)

- **Questions**: Subject + đã + Verb + Object + chưa?
  - **Example**: Chị đã làm việc này chưa? (Have you done this work yet?)
    - **Chị** (you) + **đã** (already) + **làm** (do) + **việc này** (this work) + **chưa** (yet)

### Situational Usage

1. **Casual Conversation**:
   - When casually mentioning past actions.
   - **Example**: Tôi đã gặp bạn ấy hôm qua. (I met him/her yesterday.)

2. **Formal Contexts**:
   - In formal settings, "đã" remains the same but the subject may change to reflect politeness.
   - **Example**: Ông đã ký hợp đồng chưa? (Have you signed the contract yet?)

3. **Expressing Experience**:
   - Often used when sharing experiences that have already happened.
   - **Example**: Tôi đã đi du lịch nhiều nơi. (I have traveled to many places.)

### Combined Example

**Sentence**: Bạn đã học xong chưa?
- **Meaning**: Have you finished studying yet?
- **Breakdown**:
  - **Bạn**: you (subject)
  - **đã**: already (past action marker)
  - **học xong**: finish studying (verb phrase)
  - **chưa**: yet (question marker at the end)

### Summary

- **Placement**: "Đã" is placed before the main verb to indicate that an action has been completed in the past.
- **Function in Questions**: "Đã" is used with "chưa" to form questions about the completion of actions.
- **Versatility**: "Đã" can be used with various verbs to express past actions or to ask about the status of an action.

### Sentence: Cô ấy đã đi làm.
- **English Translation**: She has gone to work.
- **Explanation**: "Đã" is used to confirm that the action of going to work has been completed.

### How to Say "She Went to Work"
To say "She went to work" in Vietnamese, you would also use the word "đã" to indicate the past action, but the context will help differentiate between "has gone" and "went." In Vietnamese, there isn't a strict distinction between "gone" and "went" as there is in English. The context of the conversation typically clarifies the meaning.

### Sentence for "She Went to Work"
**Sentence**: Cô ấy đã đi làm.
- **English Translation**: She went to work.
- **Explanation**: "Đã" is used here to indicate a past action that is completed. The same sentence structure is used for both "has gone to work" and "went to work," with the context providing the difference.

### Contextual Differences
- **She has gone to work**: This typically implies that she is currently at work, emphasizing her current location as a result of the action.
  - **Vietnamese Sentence**: Cô ấy đã đi làm.
  - **Contextual Clarification**: If you want to emphasize that she is currently at work, you might add "rồi" (already) for additional clarity.
    - **Example**: Cô ấy đã đi làm rồi. (She has already gone to work.)

- **She went to work**: This usually focuses on the past action itself, without necessarily implying her current location.
  - **Vietnamese Sentence**: Cô ấy đã đi làm.
  - **Contextual Clarification**: The same sentence is used, but the surrounding context of the conversation will indicate that you are talking about a past event.
    - **Example**: Hôm qua, cô ấy đã đi làm. (Yesterday, she went to work.)

### Additional Examples to Clarify
1. **She has gone to work**:
   - **Sentence**: Cô ấy đã đi làm rồi.
   - **Explanation**: Adding "rồi" emphasizes that she is currently at work as a result of having gone.

2. **She went to work**:
   - **Sentence**: Cô ấy đã đi làm sáng nay.
   - **English Translation**: She went to work this morning.
   - **Explanation**: Adding "sáng nay" (this morning) provides the context that the action happened in the past.

### Summary
- **Gone to work** and **went to work** both use "đã" to indicate a completed action.
- **Context** is key in differentiating between the two meanings in Vietnamese.
- Adding **rồi** can emphasize the result of the action, making it clearer that she is currently at work.
- Adding **time phrases** (like "hôm qua," "sáng nay") can specify that the action happened in the past, clarifying the meaning.
